Four AI concepts, explained.

Agent, skill, connector, subagent. Pick one to see what it does, with a simple example.

Choose an AI concept

An agent works toward a goal.

It can choose steps, use available tools, look at the results, and decide what to do next. A fixed automation follows preset steps; an agent can adapt its approach. Products use the term differently. Agent definition ↗

“Find two possible itineraries, compare them with our budget, and bring me a recommendation.”

In an app with suitable tools, it could search for activities, compare travel times, and revise the schedule. You review the result before booking.

Remember: the agent is the one doing the task.

A skill is a reusable playbook.

It bundles instructions and sometimes resources or code for a particular kind of task. The assistant can load a relevant skill when needed. This is a way to reuse a method, rather than retrain the model. Skills explained ↗

A “family itinerary” skill could say: include nap breaks, group nearby stops, and finish with a packing list.

A single request can say those things too. A skill becomes useful when you want to repeat the same method. It does not, by itself, grant access to your calendar.

Remember: the skill tells it how to do a type of task.

A connector links an app you use.

With your authorization, it lets the assistant retrieve information or perform supported actions in another service. The connector’s capabilities and your permissions determine what it can do. Connector guide ↗

A calendar connection could provide your available dates. A file connection could retrieve your saved travel notes.

That saves copying details between apps. Connecting a calendar is different from teaching the AI how your family likes to travel.

Remember: the connector provides access.

A subagent takes part of the work.

A lead agent can delegate a smaller task to another AI worker and combine its findings with other work. Some systems let these helpers work at the same time. Extra agents also add coordination and usage costs. How delegation works ↗

One helper researches museums, another outdoor activities; the lead agent combines their findings into a plan.

This depends on the product. Typing “use three subagents” into an ordinary chat does not guarantee that separate agents run.

Remember: a subagent handles a piece of the task.

Example: a travel agent.

You describe the trip in a text conversation. The agent asks what’s missing, searches through connected tools, and brings back options that fit.

A worked example: the conversation and prices below are made up to show how the pieces work together.

YOU

“Help me find a good flight deal from New York to Lisbon.”

TRAVEL AGENT

“What dates and year? How many travelers? What’s your round-trip budget per person, and in which currency? Can your dates move? What cabin, and any airport, stop, or baggage preferences?”

YOU

“Two adults, October 10–17, 2026. Up to US$700 each, round trip. JFK or Newark, dates flexible by two days. Economy, nonstop if possible, with one checked bag each.”

TRAVEL AGENT

“I’ll compare those dates and nearby dates, and check the total with bags. I can ask one helper to explore date combinations and another to compare the returned fares.”

AFTER THE SEARCH · EXAMPLE RESULTS

“Among the results returned, the best match is a US$680 nonstop round trip per person, October 11–18, with a checked bag. A US$620 option also includes a checked bag, but has a long stop. Both fit your budget; the nonstop saves travel time.”

YOU

“Actually, we need to be home by October 17.”

TRAVEL AGENT

“I’ll narrow the return dates and search again. Before any purchase, you’ll review the itinerary, current total, and fare rules, then explicitly approve.”

What’s happening behind the chat?

Agent · keeps the whole request together
It asks questions, remembers the constraints in this conversation, chooses what to search, and updates the recommendation when you change your mind.
Skill · supplies the comparison method
A reusable “compare flights” playbook could say: check dates and airports, include baggage and fees, compare stops and travel time, and explain the tradeoffs.
Connector · gets information from the travel service
An API is a way for software to request information from another service. A connector lets the agent use it. For example, Soar offers a flight-search MCP connection. MCP is a shared standard for connecting AI tools. The travel service supplies search results; the agent compares them.
Subagents · handle smaller jobs
One helper could search flexible dates. After the results arrive, another could check baggage and fare conditions against your preferences. They report back to the main agent, which combines the findings. Each helper needs access to the tools and details its job requires.

You keep talking to one agent. It coordinates the tools and any helpers.

“Best” means best for your priorities among the offers searched. A cheaper ticket can cost more with bags or a long stop. Prices and availability can change, so a real agent must recheck the selected offer before purchase. Searching is separate from permission to book. How flight search works ↗

The real examples behind this explanation

Google’s Travel Concierge sample uses a main agent with specialist helpers for planning, flights, hotels, and itineraries. Its flight search, reservations, and payments are simulated. It demonstrates how agents coordinate; our conversation above is a separate teaching example.

Google also shows how users can talk to the agent through text in Google Workspace. A chat interface is the front door; the agent and its tools do the work behind it.

Soar’s MCP documentation provides a real flight-search connection example. Its flight-search explanation (updated September 7, 2026) describes comparison and offer checks. A working travel assistant needs its own supported integrations, permissions, and booking controls. Soar documents booking as a separate flow with user approval (published September 2, 2026). This page is an explanation and does not run searches or make bookings.

Primary sources checked September 10, 2026. The reusable skill and helper roles above are a proposed design, not a claim that Google or Soar implements this exact workflow.
